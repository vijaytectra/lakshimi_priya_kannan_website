About Dr. Kannan

Twenty years.
One community.

She trained in Chennai, completed her internal medicine residency in West Texas, and finished her hematology and oncology fellowship at UT Southwestern in Dallas. She has practised in the same part of Dallas ever since — long enough to have treated people through recurrence, remission and everything in between.

That continuity is the point. In oncology, treatment runs for months or years, and the relationship carries as much weight as the protocol. A single-physician practice is the only structure that can promise that the doctor who explains your diagnosis is the doctor still adjusting your treatment a year later.

Why three certifications matter

Cancer is rarely
the only thing wrong.

Most medical oncologists hold one board certification. Dr. Kannan holds three — which changes what can be handled in a single room rather than referred out and lost between offices.

Medical oncology — the tumour: staging, systemic therapy, genomic-guided decisions.

Hematology — the blood: anemias, low counts, clotting and bleeding. The problems that most often interrupt cancer treatment.

Internal medicine — everything else you already live with, and how it interacts with treatment.

How treatment decisions get made

Test first. Then treat.

Tumour biology now tells us far more than a stage and a scan ever did. Every plan here follows the same four steps — including the one most practices skip.

01 · Test

Tumour biology, genomic and biomarker testing — before a plan is written, not after one fails.

02 · Interpret

Results read against current NCCN and ASCO guidance, and against everything else on your chart.

03 · Plan

Written down and handed to you — what it is, why it was chosen, and what the alternatives were.

04 · Reassess

Including the question asked least often — when it is safe to stop. Dr. Kannan is a participating site contact for the Breast Cancer Index registry, which studies exactly this decision.

It is exactly enough.
